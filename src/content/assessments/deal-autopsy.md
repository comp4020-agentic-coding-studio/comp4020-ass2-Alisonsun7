## The brief

> Find one real promotion that looks like a good deal, and show exactly why
> it is — or isn't.

Pick a real advertised promotion (a screenshot or link is fine as evidence).
Identify:

- the advertised claim ("50% off", "buy one get one half price", etc.)
- the reference price it's measured against, and how you'd verify it
- anything missing from the headline: exclusions, minimum spend, expiry,
  shipping
- the actual effective saving, computed, not eyeballed
- which framing techniques from Week 3 are doing work on the reader

Then reach a judgement: is this a genuinely good price, a mediocre one
dressed up, or something in between — and say exactly what evidence gets you
there. "Marketing is misleading" is not a conclusion this brief accepts on
its own; you have to show the arithmetic that makes it true or false for
this specific promotion.

## What you submit

A short written autopsy (roughly 500–800 words, or equivalent in a
structured format) covering the five points above, with the promotion's
source cited or screenshotted. Any invented numbers used to fill a gap in
public information must be clearly labelled as an assumption, not presented
as the retailer's own figure.
