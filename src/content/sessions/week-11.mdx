## Core question

> How much effort is it rational to spend in order to save money?

Week 10 priced the *uncertainty* of waiting. This week prices the *time*
waiting takes. Search cost, travel cost, and time spent watching for a
lower price are all real costs — they just aren't denominated in dollars
until you choose a value for your own hour.

## Opportunity cost of time

If your time is worth $20/hour doing anything else you'd rather be doing,
then two hours spent comparison-shopping to save $15 has already lost money
before you've bought anything: $40 of time against $15 of saving. This isn't
a claim that comparison-shopping is pointless — it's a claim that it has a
break-even point, and that point is calculable.

## The sunk-cost trap

Hours already spent searching feel like they should count toward the
decision — "I've already spent three hours on this, I might as well keep
going" — but they are a **sunk cost**: spent regardless of what you decide
next, and therefore irrelevant to what you should decide next. The only
hours that belong in the forward-looking calculation are the ones you
haven't spent yet.

## The $249 headphones

The shopper from Week 10 has already spent three hours reading reviews and
comparing listings. That's sunk. What matters now is: given the estimated
30% chance of a 15% sale, 14 days of runway, and roughly two more hours of
watching required to catch the sale if it happens — is it worth it? Change
the value of an hour in the tool above and watch the recommendation flip.
