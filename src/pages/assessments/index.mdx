# Assessment

There are three pieces of graded work, weighted 20/30/50. Each one matches a
stage of the course. [Deal Autopsy](/assessments/deal-autopsy/) is about
seeing one real promotion clearly. [Beat the Shopping
Cart](/assessments/beat-the-shopping-cart/) is about calculating what a messy
cart really costs. [Build a Buying Engine](/assessments/build-a-buying-engine/)
is the final task: build a tool or model that gives advice and explains it.
The later tasks are worth more because they use more of the course.

| Assessment | Stage | Main evidence | What changes from the previous task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Deal Autopsy](/assessments/deal-autopsy/) | SEE | One real promotion, its claim, source, reference price and restrictions | Students stop treating the sale badge as evidence by itself. |
| [Beat the Shopping Cart](/assessments/beat-the-shopping-cart/) | CALCULATE | A shopping cart, rule sheet, option table and true-cost comparison | Students move from reading one claim to working through several rules at once. |
| [Build a Buying Engine](/assessments/build-a-buying-engine/) | DECIDE | A working tool or model, example runs and a written explanation of assumptions | Students turn the semester into advice they can defend. |

Across all three assessments, marks come from clear reasoning. A lower number
is not enough: students must show what they counted, what they left out, and
why their final judgement follows.
