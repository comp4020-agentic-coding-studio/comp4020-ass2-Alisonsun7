# Assessment

Three pieces of graded work, weighted 20/30/50, and each one mirrors a stage
of the course: [Deal Autopsy](/assessments/deal-autopsy/) is a SEE-stage
exercise in reading one real promotion honestly; [Beat the Shopping
Cart](/assessments/beat-the-shopping-cart/) is a CALCULATE-stage exercise in
modelling overlapping discount rules without buying your way into a worse
outcome; [Build a Buying Engine](/assessments/build-a-buying-engine/) is the
DECIDE-stage capstone, a system that has to explain a recommendation rather
than just produce one. The weighting rises with the stage: later decisions
depend on everything the earlier ones taught.

| Assessment | Stage | Main evidence | What changes from the previous task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Deal Autopsy](/assessments/deal-autopsy/) | SEE | One real promotion, its claim, source, reference price and restrictions | Students stop treating the sale badge as evidence by itself. |
| [Beat the Shopping Cart](/assessments/beat-the-shopping-cart/) | CALCULATE | A constrained cart, rule sheet, option table and effective-cost comparison | Students move from reading one claim to modelling several rules at once. |
| [Build a Buying Engine](/assessments/build-a-buying-engine/) | DECIDE | A working model or artefact, example runs and a written explanation of assumptions | Students turn the semester into a decision system that can defend its recommendation. |

Across all three assessments, the mark is for traceable reasoning. A lower
number is not enough: students must show what was counted, what was ignored,
and why the final judgement follows from the model.
